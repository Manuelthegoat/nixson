Do these three things first — before you hire anyone

They are free, and they matter more than anything we can do later.

  1. 01

    Tell your bank today

    Card and transfer disputes have short statutory windows. Ask them to attempt a recall and to record the date you reported it.

  2. 02

    File an official report

    Reporting to the police and the relevant national fraud body costs nothing and creates the reference number every later step depends on. Where to report.

  3. 03

    Preserve everything

    Keep messages, receipts, wallet addresses and transaction IDs exactly as they are. Screenshot before anything gets deleted.

What we do

Investigation and evidence, not promises

Nobody can guarantee that stolen money comes back. What can be done is establish the facts quickly and put them in front of the parties who have the power to freeze, recall or prosecute.

Trace the funds

Follow payments through bank rails, payment processors and public blockchains to the accounts, exchanges or merchants that received them.

Identify the counterparty

Open-source and commercial-database research to attribute accounts, companies, domains and phone numbers to real, servable entities.

Package the case

A written report with an exhibit index, prepared to the standard a bank's fraud team, a regulator or a prosecutor expects to receive.

Escalate the complaint

Structured complaints to the receiving institution, the payment scheme, the financial ombudsman or the relevant regulator in the jurisdiction concerned.

Support legal action

Work alongside your own solicitor on freezing orders, disclosure applications and civil claims. We are investigators — we do not provide legal advice.

Tell you when to stop

If the trail ends at an unregulated exchange or a jurisdiction with no realistic route to recovery, we say so in writing rather than billing you to keep looking.

Warning

If someone has already contacted you offering to get your money back

Be extremely careful. Approaching fraud victims with an unsolicited recovery offer is itself one of the most common follow-up scams. Lists of people who have already lost money are traded between criminal groups precisely because those people are the easiest to approach a second time.

We do not cold-call, cold-email or message people on social media. If someone claiming to be from Cairn contacts you out of the blue, it is not us — please tell us so we can report it.
